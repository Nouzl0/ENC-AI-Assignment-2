ANSWER: The true half-life (Cambridge half-life) of carbon-14 is 5,730 ± 40 years [1], [2]. In radiocarbon dating conventions, the older "Libby half-life" value of 5,568 years is still conventionally used to calculate raw radiocarbon ages before calibration [1].

CLAIMS: C1: Carbon-14 has a half-life of 5,730 ± 40 years [1]. C2: The U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology recognizes the half-life of carbon-14 as about 5,730 years [2].
