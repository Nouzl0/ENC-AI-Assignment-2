ANSWER: The currently accepted half-life of carbon-14 is 5700 ± 30 years according to nuclear data evaluations (including the National Nuclear Data Center and the Decay Data Evaluation Project). An older value of 5730 ± 40 years, adopted by the radiocarbon dating community in 1962, is still sometimes cited.

CLAIMS:
C1: The half-life of carbon-14 is 5700 ± 30 years. [1][2][3]
C2: A 1962 radiocarbon conference adopted 5730 ± 40 years, which remains used in that community. [4]
C3: One independent evaluation of measured values gives a weighted average of 5686 ± 40 years (or 5699 ± 20 years from the five most recent measurements). [4]
C4: Conventional radiocarbon ages historically used the “Libby half-life” of 5568 ± 30 years for consistency with early results, even though it differs from the physical half-life. [2][4]
